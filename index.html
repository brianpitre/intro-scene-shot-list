The film opens in the present, inside the “all of this” Pastor John is asked about. His scripted voiceover plays over one Sunday morning: Pastors John and Leslie park, walk in the back door, meet the team in the green room, look over the service flows, pray, and John walks out to preach. Then the montage takes over and lands on The Life Church: 30 Years of Faithful Ministry.

The scene is built on four doors: the car door, the back door, the green-room door, the backstage door. Each one is a cut point and a step further inside. The camera follows: it moves behind or beside them, and from ahead it waits for them, all the way to the last door, when John walks into the light. Shutter1/60 (144°) indoors · 1/48 (180°) outside Memphis mains is 60 Hz, so the ripple is 120 Hz: 1/60 and 1/120 integrate whole cycles and are safe at any frame rate; 1/48 and 1/96 are not. At 24 the blur difference between 144° and 180° is invisible. At 48 fps indoors: 1/60 (288°) for walks and the circle, 1/120 (144°) for inserts, never 1/96: a 180° shutter at 48 alternates bright and dark frames under ripple. Test first: lock off on a wall at 1/48 for ten seconds and watch the waveform. If it holds still, the fixtures are clean and 180° is yours everywhere. Ground rules

  • Follow, don't lead. When the camera moves, it moves behind or beside them. From ahead it holds still and lets them walk into the frame. The big reverse is 4.8.
  • Doors are the cuts. Hold on every door for five seconds after it closes. The edit will use the dark.
  • Slow or still. Gimbal pushes at walking pace, locked frames on sticks. No whip pans, no handheld drift.
  • Faces, then hands, then the room. One clean wide per block for geography; everything else tight.
  • Heads and tails. Roll five seconds before the action and hold five after. Three takes of every walk.
  • Let it be real. They do what they do on a Sunday. Nobody looks at the lens. The first greeting is the one; cover it with both cameras.

Lensing

Komodo-X S35 · 27.03 mm wide · ≈1.33× vs full frame

Will the two zooms do it?

Yes for about four shots in five. The 24–70 is the A-camera lens for the green room, the review, the mic-up and the walk-with. The 70–200 is the B-camera lens for everything compressed and candid. The gap is at the wide end.

The one lens to add

  • Canon RF 15–35 ƒ/2.8L IS (20–47 mm feel). The hallway follow, the green-room wide and the prayer circle all live under 24 mm on this sensor. One borrow or rental fixes the whole gap.
  • Fallbacks. RF 14–35 ƒ/4L IS (cheaper; it leans on in-camera distortion correction the Komodo doesn't apply, so expect barrel at 14–16). Sigma 18–35 ƒ/1.8 Art on the EF-to-RF adapter from the Komodo box: the classic S35 doc lens, ƒ/1.8 helps backstage, no IS. Any 16–18 mm prime also works.
  • Nice to have. One fast prime for backstage: RF 50 ƒ/1.2L (67 feel) or RF 35 ƒ/1.8. The mic-up and the glow along the door edge at ƒ/1.2–1.8 are worth it. Without it, 24–70 at ƒ/2.8 and ISO 1600 is fine.
  • If no wide shows up. Lean long. Shoot the hallway from the far end on the 70–200. Shoot the green room as mediums and details, not one wide. Put the gimbal at 24, low and close, and accept tighter follow frames: intimacy instead of geography. It still cuts.

Focus, IS and support

  • AF is an assist, not a tracker. The RF L zooms talk to the Komodo-X (iris, IS, AF), but it is not Canon-body face tracking. On the moving shots: ƒ/4–5.6, focus set at the working distance, and the operator keeps that distance. Use AF to re-acquire between takes.
  • IS on for the monopod and any handheld. IS off on the tripod. On the gimbal, test one pass each way; IS can fight the gimbal and jitter.
  • Breathing. The 24–70 breathes visibly on a rack. Do the clock-to-John rack (3.6) on the 70–200 instead.
  • Global shutter. No skew on the car, the walks or the LED wall, so the pans and the fast moves are safe. Flicker is still about shutter speed, hence 1/60 indoors.
  • Reframe room. 6K to a 4K finish is a free 1.5× punch-in. Frame the long-lens shots a little loose and tighten in the cut.

The lot

Sunrise is about 7:00. The light that reads as a Sunday morning is gone by 8:30, so this block goes first. Park the car so the sun is behind or beside them on the walk to the door: rim light and long shadows, never a flat frontal sun.

Pickups and texture

Twenty minutes that save the edit. None of these need the cast.

Sound

the VO covers most of it; the cold open and the prayer don't

What to record

  • Lav on John from the car. Into a recorder, timecode jammed with the cameras. The greetings, the flow review and the prayer are the takes that may be used clean, in this film or the ending.
  • Second lav on Parker if there is one: the flow review is a two-hander.
  • Shotgun on the A camera for reference and for the natural sound of the cold open: car doors, footsteps on the lot, the back door, the hall.
  • Room tone in every space, thirty seconds, everyone still (shot 5.5).
  • Slate or clap every take if timecode isn't jammed. Two cameras and a recorder will not line themselves up in Premiere without it.

Staging

a Sunday on a Wednesday

Making Wednesday look like Sunday

Everything in frame has to say Sunday morning. Walk every space before call and pull the Wednesday out of it.

Cast and wardrobe

  • John and Leslie in what they would wear to preach. Their own car, clean. Whatever they carry in on a Sunday (Bible, iPad, bag, coffee) and the same items in the same hands for every take.
  • The team in Sunday clothes, lanyards or in-ears if they wear them. Phones away.
  • John's mic: the real pack and headset, and the audio tech who normally does it.
  • Continuity: jackets on or off, cups, which hand holds the Bible. One person watches it.

Spaces and screens

  • The back door unlocked and the alarm off. Nothing propped in it.
  • The countdown running on the green-room display (and any lobby or backstage screen that could land in frame), showing a plausible 10–15 minutes; reset between takes.
  • The service flow printed for a real Sunday, or on the iPad, with no Wednesday date readable in the insert.
  • The green room dressed as on a Sunday: coffee, food, the usual. Clear the clutter that isn't.
  • The auditorium in its pre-service look: house at walk-in level, stage wash and LED wall lit with real content, a touch of haze for the reveal. The production team needs to be in place for Block 04.
  • Hallway and backstage: a hidden LED tube at the far end of the hall for an edge; a small source motivated by the stage through the backstage door; otherwise the practicals as they are.

Weather. Check the forecast the night before. If the lot is a washout, shoot the arrival under the canopy if there is one, or move Block 01 to Thursday morning before the sit-down and shoot indoors first on Wednesday.

Before you strike

Wrap check

  • Four doors, four holds. Car door, back door (both sides), green-room door, backstage door (both sides), each with five seconds of hold after it closes.
  • The hard sync. Do you have the team turning to greet them, from inside the room, with sound, at 24?
  • The prayer at real time on both cameras, with the lav running.
  • The reveal from behind at 24 and at 48, three takes each, exposed for the stage.
  • John and Leslie walking into the service together, twice.
  • One clean wide per block and far more tight than wide everywhere else.
  • Room tone in all four spaces.
  • Both cards and the recorder offloaded and verified before anything leaves the building.
